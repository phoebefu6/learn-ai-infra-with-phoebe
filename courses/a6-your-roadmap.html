Part 1 · the funding order, with the evidence for each rule

Sequencing that avoids shelfware 12 min live

Six rules. Each one is a decision a sponsor makes, each one has a source behind it from earlier in this course, and each one is regularly broken in the same predictable way. Read them as constraints on the order of spend, not as a maturity model.

Storage and catalog first, even though they unblock nobody. No one's job title is "uses the object store," so these are the easiest layers to defer and the most expensive to retrofit. Everything above them inherits whatever you decided here, including your exit cost.

One persona fully unblocked beats five half-served. A data engineer who can actually ship is a working reference for the next funding round. Five personas at 60% produce five sets of complaints and no proof.

Semantic layer before self-serve, always. This is the rule the whole course exists to teach, and it is the one with a measurement attached: 9.50% accuracy on an empty configuration, 69.23% once column descriptions existed. Same product, same benchmark. Buying the top layer first does not fail loudly - it answers confidently and wrongly.

Fund the metadata work explicitly. It is the real project. The licence is the small number, and the descriptions, ownership and definitions are the part nobody scopes, which is exactly why it does not get done.

Every layer gets a named owner or it decays. Birmingham's audit found process failures - governance, oversight, vendor management - not technical ones. Ownership is the cheapest control in this entire course and the one most often left to "we will sort that out later."

Measure adoption per persona, not platform uptime. Uptime is a number your platform team can hit while nobody uses the thing. Adoption per persona is the only metric that notices shelfware early enough to act on it.

LiveWhy the gates matter more than the quarters4 min▶

The quarters will slip. Every roadmap's quarters slip, and a sponsor who defends dates rather than gates spends the year losing an argument. The gates are the part that must not move, because each one is the condition that makes the next quarter worth funding at all.

  • A gate is a sentence you can say in a budget meeting. "We are not opening self-serve until the twenty metrics finance argues about are defined and owned" is defensible. "Q3" is not.
  • A gate converts a vague risk into a visible decision. If the business chooses to pass a gate early, that is legitimate - but it is now a recorded choice with a named owner, not an accident discovered eighteen months later.
  • Gates make shelfware loud. A layer that cannot pass its adoption gate is a layer to stop paying for, and that conversation is much easier while the renewal is still ahead of you.
Self-studyThe uncomfortable counterweight to building this yourself3 min read▶

One finding deserves to sit next to your roadmap rather than be quietly left out of it. MIT's Project NANDA (August 2025) reported that 95% of enterprise generative-AI pilots deliver no measurable P&L return. State the sample size whenever you quote it, because it is thin: 52 executive interviews, 153 leader surveys and 300 public deployments. That is a directional finding, not a measurement.

Its least-quoted result is the one that belongs in a roadmap discussion: buying from specialist vendors succeeded roughly 67% of the time, while internal builds succeeded at about a third of that rate. If you are proposing to build, that number is the objection you should raise yourself, before someone else does.

The honest reconciliation with everything else in this course: buy the layers where a specialist has a real product, build only where your own semantics make you genuinely different, and be suspicious of any plan where the build list is longer than the buy list.

Part 2 · workshop · covers arXiv 2606.08266, a 15-year data-lake retrospective

Is your platform already degrading? 12 min live

An academic retrospective on fifteen years of data lakes gives the clearest available account of how a funded platform turns into an archive nobody queries. Its central idea is governance debt: the deferred work of ownership, metadata and quality, which compounds exactly like technical debt and is even less visible on a budget line.

What this source is, precisely A qualitative synthesis of 64 documentary sources plus roughly 500 field reality checks across Morocco and West Africa, covering 2010 to 2025. It is qualitative and regional - not a survey, and the percentages below are diagnostic thresholds the authors propose, not measured population statistics. Use them as a checklist you run on your own estate, not as an industry benchmark to quote.
LiveThe five-stage degradation, and where organizations recognize themselves5 min▶

The pattern the authors describe runs in one direction, and each stage looks locally reasonable at the time.

Rapid ingestion. Land everything, decide later. Genuinely the right call for a while, and it is measured as progress.

Governance deferral. Ownership, descriptions and quality checks are postponed because nothing is visibly broken yet.

Trust erosion. Two teams get different answers from the same lake. People start caveating numbers in meetings.

Retreat to warehouses. Teams quietly rebuild curated copies they can trust. Spend goes up, the lake's usage goes down, and nobody reports this as a failure.

Archive state. The platform still runs and still bills. It is now storage with a login page.

Notice that the loudest symptom - the retreat - arrives two stages after the actual cause. By the time a sponsor sees duplicated warehouses appearing, the decision that produced them is roughly two years old.

The same work proposes five indicators that can be measured against a real estate rather than argued about. Run them this quarter. They need a metadata query and an access log, not a consulting engagement.

IndicatorWarning thresholdWhat it is really telling you
Datasets without an owner>50%Nobody is accountable for correctness. Every quality question becomes an investigation.
Metadata completeness<40%The layer above cannot work. This is the same deficit that produced 9.50% instead of 69.23%.
Dormant datasets>40%You are paying storage and attention on data no decision depends on.
Duplication>20%Teams have already started rebuilding their own trusted copies. Stage four is under way.
Automated quality validationnoneEvery quality problem is found by a person, downstream, usually in a meeting.
LiveWhat to do with a bad score4 min▶

A bad score is not a reason to replatform, and the reflex to do so is how organizations pay twice for the same governance debt. Three moves in order:

  • Assign ownership before doing anything else. It is the cheapest of the five and it unblocks the other four, because an unowned dataset has nobody to ask about its metadata.
  • Retire the dormant tier deliberately. A dormant dataset with no owner and no queries is a candidate for archive, and the exercise usually pays for a chunk of the metadata work.
  • Put metadata completeness on the same report as spend. If it is not next to a cost number, it is not a management metric, and it will lose to whatever is.

Then re-measure in a quarter. These five indicators are cheap enough to run repeatedly, which is the property that makes them useful - a diagnostic you run once is an opinion.

Part 3 · workshop · covers Michelin's 3-year retrospective, 2022-05-20

Five questions, and the federation lesson 10 min live

These five questions take about ninety seconds to ask and cannot be answered with a demo. Ask them in that order. The pattern of what a vendor answers precisely and what they answer warmly tells you most of what the evaluation would have told you in six weeks.

Ask thisWhat a good answer looks like
"Show me the accuracy number and the benchmark."A figure, a named benchmark, and a published method. Most of this category publishes no accuracy figure at all - treat that absence as informative rather than neutral.
"What is the egress cost of leaving?"A rate, not a reassurance. Reading your own data out is a metered line item on some platforms and free on others, and it decides whether your exit is real.
"Is licensing per used unit or per installed unit?"Precision, immediately. Per-installed-GPU licensing is the line first-pass budgets miss, and the gap between used and installed is where surprise renewals live.
"What stops a team going around this control?"Architecture, not policy. If the honest answer is that nothing technically prevents it, the control governs the people who were already compliant.
"Which of my eight layers does this actually fill?"Two or three named layers, with the rest disclaimed. A product that claims all eight is describing a category, not a capability.
LiveMichelin: federation beat mandate, with a number5 min▶

Michelin published a three-year retrospective on their own blog in May 2022, and it is the most honest data-organization retro in the source set because it leads with what went wrong.

  • Local teams bypassed the central tools and built redundant BI. Not a rumour and not an outside assessment - their own account of what happened.
  • Michelin chose federation over mandate. Rather than enforcing central tooling, they made the central data products good enough to be worth adopting.
  • The result: 60% of the data used in local solutions came from central products by year two. That is adoption, measured, without a mandate.
  • They also reported that semantic alignment across domains was harder than expected, and that initially they could measure only platform usage, not the quality of individual data products.

The leadership lesson is uncomfortable and specific: a mandate makes bypass invisible, while federation makes adoption measurable. If teams are going around your platform today, the productive question is what your platform would have to be for them to choose it - not which policy would forbid the alternative. And it connects directly back to the chokepoint test: a control people can route around is a suggestion, so make the compliant path the easy one.

Real world

Worth pairing with the original definition of data mesh, which is routinely dropped from the summaries: an intentionally designed distributed architecture, under centralized governance and standardization for interoperability, enabled by a shared self-serve infrastructure. Most "data mesh failed" stories are stories about organizations that kept the decentralization and discarded the governance and the shared platform - which is to say, they did not do the thing that failed.

LiveWriting your roadmap: the one-page shape5 min▶

Take fifteen minutes and write this. One page, four rows, and it will survive contact with a budget committee better than any deck you commission.

  • Per quarter: which layers get funded, which persona is fully unblocked by the end of it, and the gate that must be passed before the next quarter starts.
  • One named owner per layer. A person, not a team. If a layer has no candidate owner, that is the item to fix before the funding request, not after.
  • The metadata line, costed separately from the licence. Make it visible enough that cutting it is a decision somebody has to defend.
  • The adoption measure per persona, and the date you first report it. Not uptime.
  • Your five swamp indicators, with today's numbers. A baseline you can re-run in ninety days is what turns this page from a plan into a management instrument.

If your roadmap has a Q4 self-serve gate, that is the course that tells you how to test whether you have passed it.

One closing note on all three. The through-line of this track has been that ordering beats selection, and evidence beats confidence. Every number on these six pages carried a source and a date, and where the evidence did not exist - as with published data-platform failures - the honest move was to say so rather than to fill the gap. Hold your own reporting to that standard and most of the sequencing takes care of itself.

Check yourself

Three questions before you go 🎯 ◐ 90 seconds

1 · Your board wants self-serve analytics live next quarter. The semantic layer is not built. What does the sequencing rule say, and on what evidence?

The bottleneck is semantic context, not the model, and the failure mode is not an error message - it is plausible SQL carrying wrong business logic, which nothing flags. Shipping first does not surface problems faster, it hides them in decisions.

2 · Which of these is one of the published swamp indicators, and what is the correct caveat when you quote it?

The five indicators are: over 50% of datasets without ownership, under 40% metadata completeness, over 40% dormant datasets, over 20% duplication, and no automated quality validation. They come from a qualitative synthesis of 64 sources plus about 500 field reality checks in Morocco and West Africa, 2010-2025.

3 · Michelin found local teams bypassing central tools and building redundant BI. What did they do, and what happened?

A mandate makes bypass invisible; federation makes adoption measurable. Michelin also reported that semantic alignment across domains was harder than expected, and that at first they could measure only platform usage rather than the quality of individual data products.
